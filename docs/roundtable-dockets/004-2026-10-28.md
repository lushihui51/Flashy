- LPD4. Home's steps to a first practice are headed "Your first practice", with how many are done as "N of 5", and Home's decks are headed "Recently practiced". "Coming soon." and "Notifications" leave the vocabulary. "Back", spoken, enters for the "<" in the row of places. "Edit card" also labels the run page's button.
  - Facts: A newcomer needs Home to show what they have done and where the steps end. Notifications led only to "Coming soon.". The "<" goes back.
  - Rejected: an untitled list of steps, which says nothing; "Getting started", which says nothing about where it ends; "Start here", which stays true after the user has started.
  - Becomes true: the words on Home, the menu, the row of places and the run page are the vocabulary's.
  - Drawings: 8, 9, 10, 18, 19
- LPD5. Every tab, every count on a practice's summary and every "Order by" choice is drawn as PDR 020's block: white, with rounded corners, a thin ink outline and a short ink shadow. The chosen one is filled in ink and pressed in, with no shadow. This holds for Your library's tabs, a deck's tabs, Practice's status tabs, the summary's counts, "Order by" and a configuration's "Each card shows … of these" counts.
  - Facts: PDR 020 draws everything a user can open or act on as a block. Tabs drawn as underlined words, "Order by" drawn as outlined pills with no shadow, and a count not chosen drawn with no outline of its own look like text that is only read.
  - Rejected: tabs as underlined words, "Order by" as outlined pills with no shadow, and a count not chosen with no outline of its own, which leaves the user unable to tell a tab or a choice from text that is only read; a configuration's counts with no shadow.
  - Becomes true: what can be tapped looks the same everywhere, choices included.
  - Drawings: 1, 3, 4, 5, 11, 13, 14, 15
- LPD6. After a tab or filter change, the new list's first rows are on screen, with the control just tapped still in view. The page moves only as far as that takes, and not at all when both are already in view. This holds for Your library's tabs, a deck's tabs, Practice's status tabs and filters, New practice's filters and the summary's counts.
  - Facts: a user changes a tab or filter to read the new list from its start. Moving the control the user just tapped away from under their finger loses their sense of place on the page.
  - Rejected: the page staying where it was, which can leave the new list's first rows above the screen; the page going back to the top, which jumps even when the control and the new list's start are both in view.
  - Becomes true: the user always meets a new list at its start, with as little movement as possible. Going back is unchanged, as PDR 017 has it.
- LPD7. How mastery, its change, the rating marks, the key and the summary's buttons look and where they sit. The mastery circle is small, with a thin ink outline, and fills in ink from the top, clockwise. It sits at the right of every row for a subject, a deck or a card, and, larger, beside the heading of a subject's page and of a deck's page. A card's change on the summary is a small round badge just off the circle's top-right, overlapping it slightly: ink with a double chevron up for up a lot; ink with a single chevron up for up; white with a grey outline and a grey dash for no change; red with a single chevron down for down; red with a double chevron down for down a lot. On a scale from 0 to 100 where 50 is no change, 80 and above is up a lot, 55 up to 80 is up, 45 to 55 is no change, 20 up to 45 is down, and below 20 is down a lot. On the summary, "Re-run" is a white button beside "Done". The counts sit under "Done", each under its kind's badge, and a chosen count is drawn as LPD5 has it. Once one or more counts are chosen, a yellow "Practice these" appears under the counts and "Done" turns white. "Order by:" and its three choices sit below the counts. A practice started from "Practice these" is named for the moment it started. A card opened from the summary starts with its name, its circle and its change badge, as on its row. Below is one record card holding "Prompt", then "Answer". Each field's row has a top line with the field's name, then its marks, then its circle and change badge, and the field's value runs at full width below that line. "Edit card", with a pencil, sits under the record card. A prompt field's mark is a solid ink dot when the attempt showed it, and an empty light-grey ring when it did not. An answer field's mark is a solid red dot for Again, a red ring with a small dot in its centre for Hard, an ink ring with a small dot in its centre for Good, a solid ink dot for Easy, and an empty light-grey ring when the attempt did not ask it. An italic "i" button with an outline and a press shadow sits on the record card's top-left corner. Tapping it opens a panel over the record card that names each mark, under Prompt and under Answer, and dims the rest of the sheet. Tapping the "i" again, or anywhere outside the panel, closes it. Wherever a rating shows as a word, its chip is red for Again, white outlined in red for Hard, white outlined in ink for Good, and ink for Easy.
  - Facts: every row on the summary ends with a ">" that opens it. A card can have several answer fields, so Hard, Good and Easy repeat across attempts and need telling apart at a glance. Answers can be long. The marks need explaining without crowding the sheet. How a change is placed on the scale is the code side's to work out.
  - Rejected: filled triangles beside the circle, too bulky; thin chevrons beside the circle, next to the row's own ">"; thin arrows beside the circle; no badge for no change; thirds on each side with only exactly 50 as no change, which makes the list busy; "a lot" as only the outer tenth, which makes it rare; the circles before and after side by side, which look alike when a practice moves a card a little; the change drawn as a slice inside the circle, too small to see at a row's size; arrows in green and red, which bring in a color PDR 015 leaves out; each value beside its marks, which squeezes a long answer; the "i" on the Prompt heading, which reads as information about the prompt; the "i" beside the card's name, on the record card's top-right corner, or above the marks; numbered attempt columns; a closing chip with the last rating, which repeats the last mark; every rating as a chip; plain ink dots for every rating but Again; ink-only shapes, which have to be learned; dots holding the rating's first letter; a color for each rating; hollow rings with no dot in the centre; square marks for prompt fields; the mastery before and after on the sheet's heading, as a grey wedge under the ink or as two circles side by side.
  - Becomes true: every circle, badge and mark has the canvas's shape, and the summary and its card sheet have the canvas's layout. A rating reads in the same colors as a mark on the sheet and as a chip on the run page.
  - Drawings: 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19

  - Rejected: the full sentence "These cards can't be practiced.   - Facts: a filter's or a picker's open list dims the page behind it, and the rating chip's list dims nothing.

- LCD3. Every mark drawn in or on a circle is one inline SVG on a fixed viewBox. This covers the mastery circle with its fill, each change badge, each prompt and rating mark, and the key's "i" with its press shadow: one component per kind of mark under frontend/src/components/ui/, its geometry from the canvas. The chevrons, the dash and the "i" are paths in that SVG, not lucide icons. A change badge is its own SVG, placed by CSS off the circle's top-right.
  - Facts: with the canvas's 1.5px outlines, a circle drawn with a CSS border snaps to the pixel grid and a mark positioned inside it by CSS does not, so the mark sits up to half a pixel off centre. The canvas draws each of these marks as one SVG. The canvas holds for exact shapes. ADR 080 governs icons as pictures, and a mark drawn as part of a circle is not one.
  - Rejected: CSS circles with marks positioned inside; CSS sizes chosen to land on whole pixels, which breaks silently when an ancestor lands on a fraction; lucide's chevrons nested in the badge, whose geometry differs from the canvas.
  - Becomes true: a mark cannot drift from its circle. ADR 080 stays as written.
  - Drawings: 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19
- LCD4. A completed practice's time on Home is the time of its last rating. Home reads the decks of completed runs, ordered by each run's latest `review_log.reviewed_at`, each deck once, the first three. A completed run with no rating counts none of its decks. It is one grouped statement, with no column and no write.
  - Facts: `practice_run` carries `created_at` only. `get_current_practice_card` marks a run completed on a read when no card is pending, for a finished run and a stranded one alike (ADR 073). `PracticeRunPage` refetches the run's state after every rating, so a finished run completes straight after its last rating. A `review_log.review_group_id` is its practice card's id.
  - Rejected: a `completed_at` column stamped when the run completes, which stamps an old stranded run now and lifts its decks to the top; a stamp by the rating that leaves nothing pending, which still needs the last-rating rule for stranded runs; `created_at`, which sorts a practice finished today by the day it started.
  - Becomes true: PDR 016's order needs no schema change.
- LCD5. Home reads one composed `GET /api/home`. It returns the steps' state, from `first_practice_at` and whether a subject, a deck, a card, a configuration and a practice exist; up to three active practices, most recent first, each with its progress; and LCD4's three decks, each with its readiness. It uses a fixed number of statements (ADR 091). `HomePage` owns it as the query `['home']`, refetched on every mount under TanStack Query's default staleness (ADR 088).
  - Facts: `GET /api/subjects`, `/api/decks`, `/api/deck_practice_configs` and `/api/practice_runs` each return a whole collection. `PracticeRunSummary` carries no progress; only each run's `GET .../state` does. No read says whether the user has any card.
  - Rejected: Home combining the existing lists, which fetches four collections for five yeses and noes plus one state read per active practice; progress on `PracticeRunSummary`, which makes every row on the Practice page pay for what only Home shows.
  - Becomes true: Home costs one request, and a write elsewhere shows on Home's next visit without invalidating `['home']`.
- LCD7. The run page keeps "answers shown" and the ratings chosen so far in its URL, tagged with their practice card. For example `/practice/{id}/run?card={practiceCardId}&shown=1&rating.{fieldId}=3`. The page reads them only when `card` is the current practice card, and writes them with `replace`. It opens the card's form with `cancelTo` and `returnTo` both set to its full address (ADR 103), and refetches its state on return.
  - Facts: `PracticeRunPage` keeps `showAnswer` and `ratings` in `useState`, and sends the ratings only when the card is submitted. `CardStandaloneForm` saves and cancels to the card's deck and reads no `returnTo` of its own. PDR 019 returns the user to the run page as they left it.
  - Rejected: `sessionStorage` keyed by the practice card, a second store of view state; the history entry's router state, which the new entry `returnTo` pushes does not carry; the card's form as an overlay on the run page, which a user sees as a different form.
  - Becomes true: a reload keeps the user's place on the card too, and nothing carries to the next card.
