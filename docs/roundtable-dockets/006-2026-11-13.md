## Product decisions

- PD5. The work of a guest, a visitor who is not logged in, lasts until they close or refresh the tab, and is then gone.
  - Facts: A guest uses the app with no account. The owner wants nothing of a guest's use to outlive the page. A phone can reload a tab on its own, a user refreshes a page that looks stuck, and PDR 031 has the user who meets a page that broke reload it in the browser.
  - Rejected: Work kept through a refresh and gone when the tab closes, and work kept in the browser until the guest clears it, because in both something of a guest's use outlives the page; kept in the browser, a guest never needs an account, and the next person at a shared computer finds the last guest's decks.
  - Becomes true: Nothing a guest makes outlives the page. A guest who refreshes, whose phone reloads the tab, or who reloads a page that broke loses their work.
- PD6. When a guest makes an account, everything they made as a guest goes into it. When a guest logs in to an account that already exists, the guest's work is dropped and that account's library is shown.
  - Facts: Under PD5 a guest loses their work on any refresh, so making an account is the one way to keep it. PDR 033 keeps a deck's name unique among its user's decks.
  - Rejected: Guest work always dropped, because the guest who made a deck and then decided to keep it would lose it at the moment they commit. Guest work always added to the account, because a guest's deck can share a name with one the account has, and practice tried as a guest would mix into the account's mastery.
  - Becomes true: A guest who makes an account finds everything they made already in it. A user who logs in to an account they already have gets that account's library, and what they made as a guest is gone.
- PD7. While a visitor is a guest, a banner in ink across the top of every page, in the form of the connection banner, says they are a guest and that logging in keeps their work. It is one line at the default text size. When the connection banner also shows, it sits above the guest's. Once a guest has made something, the browser asks before the tab is closed or refreshed.
  - Facts: A guest's work is gone when the tab closes or refreshes, and making an account through "Log in" keeps it. PDR 054 gives yellow to the one main action a screen is for, and ink to what is said. "Save" is the word for finishing a form. "Log in" sits in the top bar directly under the banner and opens the way to make an account. The banner's words fit one line on a phone at the default text size.
  - Rejected: Telling the guest nothing, or once when they first make something, because a guest who loses their work concludes the app lost it. A quiet grey line, because it is easy to miss. The browser's question alone, because it comes only at the moment of leaving, in the browser's words, and not when a phone reloads the tab. A yellow banner, because a form would then show three yellow things. Words saying the work is not saved, because they sit above a form's "Save". A button to make an account in the banner, because "Log in" already opens that way. Words telling the guest to make an account, because they leave the guest to find that "Log in" is the way to do it.
  - Becomes true: A guest reads on every page who they are and how to keep what they make, and gets a last chance before a close or refresh they caused. A guest gives up one line of the screen on every page. The banner's words are untrue for a guest who logs in to an account that already exists, and PD8 tells that guest so.
  - Drawings: 1, 2
- PD8. When a guest who has made something logs in to an account that already exists, the app asks before the guest's work is dropped. Cancel leaves them a guest, with their work still there. Discard takes them into their account.
  - Facts: Under PD6, logging in to an account that already exists drops a guest's work, and PD7's banner says logging in keeps it. The app asks before a user leaves a form with unsaved changes, with "Cancel" and "Discard".
  - Rejected: A warning before the log-in dialog opens for every guest with work, because the newcomer making a first account loses nothing and must read and dismiss it. No warning, because the banner's promise is then broken silently. Adding the guest's work to the existing account, because PD6 rejects it.
  - Becomes true: Only the user whose guest work is about to be dropped is asked, at the moment it applies, and keeps it by backing out. A newcomer goes from the log-in dialog straight into their new account with their work in it.

## Code decisions

- CD1. A guest's work is held in the database, as the rows of an `app_user` of its own that has no Clerk identity, read and written through the same routes, services and database operations as a logged-in user's.
  - Facts: Every route handler but `GET /api/health` takes `CurrentUserDep`. Ownership hangs from `app_user.id` on `deck`, `practice` and `review_log`. Mastery arithmetic, run generation, the deletion closure, Home's read and a run's day are worked out on the server (ADR 116, ADR 112, ADR 118, ADR 123, ADR 114). Clerk offers no user without credentials. Nothing of a guest's use outliving the page means nothing a person can find again, and a row may remain in the database after the tab closes.
  - Rejected: A TypeScript implementation answering the API in the browser, because every rule is written twice and drifts with no test failing, and making an account would import reviews stamped by the browser's clock, against ADR 019. The backend's Python run in the browser on a PostgreSQL built for the browser, because both download before the first page works. Tables or a schema apart from accounts' data, because every migration runs twice and moving a guest's work into an account is a copy with every id remapped. A guest as a Clerk user, because Clerk has none.
  - Becomes true: A guest's use works as a logged-in user's with no route, service or page changed. A new account takes a guest's work by a change of owner on rows already there. A guest's rows stay after the tab closes until something removes them. Anyone can make the server write rows with no account.
- CD2. `app_user.first_practice_at` is renamed `first_run_at`, an instant stamped by the server while it is null, inside `cut_run`'s transaction in `app/services/run.py`, and written by nothing else. `GET /api/home` returns `steps` as null once it is set. Until then `steps` carries `has_deck`, `has_card` and `practice_id`, the id of the user's most recently made practice or null. The third step is ticked when `practice_id` is set, and the fourth step calls `POST /api/practices/{practice_id}/runs` with it and opens the run. The migration renames the column, sets it to the instant of each user's earliest run, and leaves it null for a user with no run.
  - Facts: PDR 057 keeps Home's steps until the user has started a run and adds a fourth step that starts a run of the practice made most recently. `create_practice` in `app/services/practice.py` stamps `first_practice_at`, and `get_home` in `app/services/home.py` ends the steps on it (ADR 123). `HomeSteps` holds `has_deck` and `has_card` alone. `cut_run` cuts every run (ADR 112). A completed run's play button on Home already calls `startRun` and opens the run. Home's way to All practices shows only once the steps have left Home, as PDR 057 means it. The app is not published.
  - Rejected: The stamp at the first practice, because a user with a practice and no run sees no steps and no runs. A `first_run_at` beside `first_practice_at`, because the first is then written and read by nothing. The name kept and stamped at the first run, because it says practice and means run. No stamp, with the steps shown while the user has no run, because they come back after a deletion. A route of its own that picks the practice, because it is a route for one button. The client picking the practice from the list of practices, because it puts a selection rule in TypeScript and loads every practice.
  - Becomes true: The steps end at the first run cut on any path and never return. Home's read carries the practice its fourth step starts. A user in the dev database who ran a practice and then deleted it sees the steps again.
- CD3. The column's width is one `@theme` token in rem, `--container-column: 40rem`, in `frontend/src/index.css`, applied in the shell alone. `AppShell` holds the top bar's contents, each banner's text and `<main>` to it, centred, and `FormBar` holds its sentence, count and buttons to it less the page's side padding. The banners' and the foot bar's bands keep the window's width. No page writes the column, and no breakpoint is used.
  - Facts: PDR 052 shows every page on a wide screen in one column in the middle of the window, about one and a half times a phone's width, and holds the top bar's contents and the foot bar's buttons to it. Drawing 1 draws the header's contents and the page at 640px, the foot bar's buttons at 608px, and both bands across the window. Every page renders inside `AppShell`'s `<main>`, and every bar at the foot is `FormBar` (ADR 127). A design size used more than once is a `@theme` token in rem (ADR 101). No element caps its width today.
  - Rejected: Each page wrapping itself, because one of fifteen routes that forgets stretches across the window with nothing failing. A cap on the app's root, because an element fixed to the viewport ignores it. A breakpoint, because a cap does nothing below its own width. A value in px, because it holds still while the text grows.
  - Becomes true: A new page sits in the column with nothing to remember. A new element fixed to the viewport spans the window unless it applies the token, and no test sees it, since jsdom does no layout (ADR 017).
  - Drawings: 1
- CD4. `SearchBar` in `frontend/src/components/shell/SearchBar.tsx` and its test file are deleted, with its mount in `AppShell`.
  - Facts: LPD2 takes the search out of the top bar until it finds something. `SearchBar` has no user but `AppShell`, and Enter in it does nothing. What a search finds and where its results appear is undecided.
  - Rejected: The component kept and not mounted, because its tests would guard a control no screen shows.
  - Becomes true: No component exists for a control no screen shows.
  - Drawings: 1, 2

## Lesser decisions

- LPD1. "Log in" is drawn as a block, with the ink outline and shadow, on every page a guest sees.
  - Facts: PDR 020 draws everything that can be tapped as a block with an ink outline and shadow. PDR 054 makes "Log in" yellow on every page a guest sees. Today "Log in" is yellow with no outline and no shadow.
  - Rejected: None.
  - Becomes true: A guest finds "Log in" drawn as a block like everything else that can be tapped.
  - Drawings: 1, 2
- LPD2. The search is taken out of the top bar until it finds something.
  - Facts: The search sits in the top bar on every page, takes typing, and does nothing when the user presses Enter. Once the app is published, every visitor meets it. What a search finds and where its results appear is undecided.
  - Rejected: Leaving it, because a control that takes typing and then does nothing reads as broken. Deciding what it finds and building it before publishing, because it pushes the launch back.
  - Becomes true: Nothing in the top bar leads nowhere. Every page on a phone gains the search's row back. The user has no search.
  - Drawings: 1, 2
