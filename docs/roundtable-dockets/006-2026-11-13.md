## Lesser decisions

- LPD1. "Log in" is drawn as a block, with the ink outline and shadow, on every page a guest sees.
  - Facts: PDR 020 draws everything that can be tapped as a block with an ink outline and shadow. PDR 054 makes "Log in" yellow on every page a guest sees. Today "Log in" is yellow with no outline and no shadow.
  - Rejected: None.
  - Becomes true: A guest finds "Log in" drawn as a block like everything else that can be tapped.
  - Drawings: 1, 2
- LPD2. The search is taken out of the top bar until it finds something.
  - Facts: The search sits in the top bar on every page, takes typing, and does nothing when the user presses Enter. Once the app is published, every visitor meets it. What a search finds and where its results appear is undecided.
  - Rejected: Leaving it, because a control that takes typing and then does nothing reads as broken. Deciding what it finds and building it before publishing, because it pushes the launch back.
  - Becomes true: Nothing in the top bar leads nowhere. Every page on a phone gains the search's row back. The user has no search.
  - Drawings: 1, 2
- LPD3. The banner that tells a guest they are looking at an example takes the form of the connection banner, and is one line at the default text size. When the connection banner also shows, it sits above the guest's.
  - Facts: PDR 053 tells a guest on every page, in a banner in ink across the top, that what they see is an example. PDR 031 puts the connection banner at the top of every page. The banner's words fit one line on a phone at the default text size.
  - Rejected: None.
  - Becomes true: A guest reads both banners in one form, the connection's first. A guest gives up one line of the screen on every page.
  - Drawings: 1, 2, 3, 4
- LPD4. The example library's runs show the days they were really run.
  - Facts: A run is named by the day it was run, written as a date, on Home, on a practice's page and as the heading of a completed run's page. The example library is the same for every visitor, so its runs were run before the app was published.
  - Rejected: Days that keep pace with the visitor's today, because the owner finds it more than a guest's look at the app is worth.
  - Becomes true: A guest reads the same dates on the example's runs whenever they visit. A guest who visits months after the app is published reads runs dated months back.
- LPD5. The example library holds a deck named "Common words", of about fifty everyday words, each card holding the word in five fields in this order: English, Chinese, Korean, French, Spanish.
  - Facts: The owner's own decks stay out of the example library. The owner wants material anybody knows. PDR 006 names a card, wherever it is listed, by the value of its deck's first field. The app's own words are English.
  - Rejected: Chinese first, because a guest who does not read Chinese cannot tell one card's row from the next. The owner's own decks, alone or beside this one, because they stay the owner's.
  - Becomes true: A guest reads every card's name in English. A guest sees a card that holds five fields. A guest who speaks one of the languages notices a wrong translation.
  - Drawings: 1, 2
- LPD6. The example library holds a second, smaller deck named "Capitals", of twenty well-known countries, each card holding three fields in this order: Country, Capital, Continent.
  - Facts: The owner wants a second, smaller deck, on something other than language, that anybody knows. PDR 035 makes a practice a named set of decks.
  - Rejected: "Common words" alone, because no practice could then hold more than one deck and the guest never sees decks gathered. A second language deck, because the owner wants something other than language. The eight planets, because eight cards give a run little to show. The first twenty elements, because a guest who has forgotten school chemistry cannot judge an answer.
  - Becomes true: A guest sees two decks with different fields in Your library, and a practice can hold both. A guest who knows no second language still finds answers they can judge.
  - Drawings: 1, 2
- LPD7. The example library holds four practices, each with completed runs. "Common words + Capitals" holds both decks: a Common words card shows English and asks Spanish, and a Capitals card shows Country and asks Capital and Continent. "Korean from Chinese and English" holds Common words: every card shows Chinese and English and asks Korean. "Two at random" holds Common words: every card shows English and asks two of the other four languages, drawn at random each time the card comes up. "French from English" holds Common words: every card shows English and asks French. Three have a run left partway: "Common words + Capitals", the most recent, at a Capitals card on its first attempt, with no card failed so far; "Korean from Chinese and English", at a card that has come back as a retry, with others still to come back; and "Two at random", at a card on its first attempt, with some cards retried earlier in the run. "French from English" has none.
  - Facts: A guest cannot move a run on, so each run left partway is one card they see. PDR 038 gives a practice at most one active run. PDR 057 lists up to three active runs on Home, and under Recently completed no run of a practice that has an active one. The owner wants a practice that asks Korean from Chinese and English, a practice that shows random draw, and several runs left partway, some with retries and some without.
  - Rejected: One run left partway, because the guest sees one card. Two, because the guest sees one card fewer than Home's Active can hold. All four, because Home shows only three and nothing under Recently completed. A practice asking all four other languages, because the owner wants Korean asked from Chinese and English in its place. "Two at random" drawing its prompt at random too, because some cards would open on a prompt in Chinese or Korean alone. One practice of both decks alone, because the guest never sees a deck asked in different ways. One practice for each deck, because no practice gathers decks.
  - Becomes true: A guest sees three different cards, one from each run left partway, and the first from Home has two answers anyone can check. A guest sees one deck asked three ways on three boards. A guest finds three runs under Active on Home and one under Recently completed.
- LCD1. The guest's banner is `ExampleBanner` in `frontend/src/components/shell/`, rendered by `AppShell` in its header, beneath `ConnectionBanner`, while `useViewer()` answers `guest`. Both banners are drawn through one frame, `Banner` in the same folder, which takes a colour, an optional icon and the words, and `ConnectionBanner` and `ExampleBanner` each give it theirs.
  - Facts: PDR 053 gives a guest a banner in ink across the top of every page, and LPD3 gives it the form of the connection banner, one line at the default text size, beneath the connection banner when both show. ADR 136 holds each banner to the column in `AppShell`'s header. `ConnectionBanner` draws its own frame today, in red with an icon, and is the app's one channel for a failure that belongs to no page (ADR 063, ADR 070).
  - Rejected: A guest's state added to `ConnectionBanner`, because that component then answers for the connection and for who is looking, and the one failure channel carries something that is not a failure. `ExampleBanner` drawing a frame of its own, because the two frames are then kept alike by hand, and LPD3 has them in one form.
  - Becomes true: The two banners share their padding, text size and width by construction. A third banner is a new caller of `Banner`. `ConnectionBanner` stays the only banner that reports a failure.
  - Drawings: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5
- LCD2. `SearchBar` in `frontend/src/components/shell/SearchBar.tsx` and its test file are deleted, with its mount in `AppShell`.
  - Facts: LPD2 takes the search out of the top bar until it finds something. `SearchBar` has no user but `AppShell`, and Enter in it does nothing. What a search finds and where its results appear is undecided.
  - Rejected: The component kept and not mounted, because its tests would guard a control no screen shows.
  - Becomes true: No component exists for a control no screen shows.
  - Drawings: 1, 2
- LCD3. The vocabulary's row for a practice gives the code names of the practice, and its two rows for a run's state each give `RunStatus` and `status` beside the value they already give.
  - Facts: The row "A named set of decks the user keeps and runs as many times as they like" reads "—" under Code, though the `practice` table, the `Practice` model, the `/api/practices` routes and the address parameter `practiceId` name its concept. The row for a run gives `run`; `Run`; `RunRead`; `/api/runs`; `runId`. The rows "A run that can still be continued" and "A run that has finished" give `active` and `completed` alone, and no row gives `RunStatus` or the `status` field that carries them. An agent finds a concept by grepping a code name in the file.
  - Rejected: The rows left as they are, because a grep for `Practice` or `RunStatus` finds no row for its concept. `RunStatus` and `status` on the run's own row, because that row is the run, and the two state rows are where the values they name already stand. A new row for a run's state, because the two state rows already are that concept, once each.
  - Becomes true: A grep for `practice`, `Practice`, `RunStatus` or `status` lands on the row of its concept. A grep for `RunStatus` returns two rows, the two states it holds.
- LCD4. The local bypass in `app/dependencies.py` stays as it is.
  - Facts: The bypass answers every request as one user only while `DEV_AUTH_USER_ID` and `ENV=development` are both set, and the app logs a warning at startup when it is on. A browser check runs under it, signed out of Clerk. ADR 132 adds a frontend switch that matches it. ADR 137 sets neither variable in production.
  - Rejected: Removing it, because a browser check would then need a real Clerk session inside a headless browser.
  - Becomes true: Local browser checks keep working as they do. A production service on which someone sets both variables answers every request as one user, and Railway's variables are the one place that could happen.
