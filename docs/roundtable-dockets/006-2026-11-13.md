## Product decisions

- PD1. A guest chooses a rating for each answer on a run's card, and the answer shows it. Pressing "Next card" dims the screen and tells them to log in.
  - Facts: Most guests come to look. Choosing a rating changes nothing in the example library; only "Next card" rates the card. A guest who chooses a rating meets the dimmed screen, and "Next card" stays grey whatever they do. A rating takes two presses, and a guest types nothing to choose one.
  - Rejected: Choosing a rating dims the screen, as PDR 058 decides, because the guest never sees a rated answer and "Next card" stays grey with nothing saying why. A guest going on through the cards with nothing kept, because they would believe they are practicing and lose it when the page loads again.
  - Becomes true: A guest sees a rated answer and "Next card" come alive. A guest learns at "Next card" that their ratings do not count. A guest still never sees the card after.
  - Drawings: 6
- PD2. Whenever the screen dims to tell a guest to log in, the banner across the top stays lit with "Log in".
  - Facts: The banner tells a guest that what they see is an example and that logging in opens all of Flashy, in white on ink.
  - Rejected: The banner dimmed with the rest of the screen, as PDR 058 decides, because "This is an example." fades at the moment it explains why the press did nothing.
  - Becomes true: A guest reads the banner at full strength while the screen is dimmed. A guest sees two lit things at the top of the dimmed screen.
  - Drawings: 3, 4, 6, 7
- PD3. The dimmed screen shows no sentence beneath "Log in"; the lit banner alone says to log in.
  - Facts: The lit banner says "This is an example. Log in to use all of Flashy." in white on ink. Small white words set straight on the dimmed page stand at about 3.5 to 1, less than small text needs.
  - Rejected: The same sentence beneath "Log in", as PDR 058 decides, because the same words would stand twice a finger's width apart, the lower ones hard to read on the dimmed page. A different sentence beneath "Log in", because it adds a second sentence to read, still small white words on the dimmed page.
  - Becomes true: A guest who presses something that would change the example library sees the screen go dark but for the banner and "Log in", and reads the reason once, in the banner. Nothing new appears with the press.
  - Drawings: 3, 4, 6, 7
