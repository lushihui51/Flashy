Four things wait on the answer: mapping a database outage to 503 on every endpoint once the frontend and API are cross-origin (ADR 072); removing the local auth bypass in `app/dependencies.py` once local testing no longer needs it; rate limiting of `GET /api/health` (ADR 072); and a startup check that the database is at alembic head (ADR 045).
  - Owner: /code-decide
  - Needed by: nothing
- Q2. Do the practice's vocabulary row and the run's rows give their code names, `practice`, `Practice` and `/practices` for a practice and `RunStatus` and `status` for a run's state?

## Product decisions

- PD1. On a wide screen, every page is shown in the phone's layout, in one column in the middle of the window, about one and a half times as wide as a phone's screen. The top bar's contents and the buttons of the bar at the foot are held to the same column. A run in progress keeps its prompt and its answers side by side.
  - Facts: Everything a user does on a phone they also do on a laptop. The app is to be published as soon as possible, and a layout made for a wide screen takes more work than publishing can wait for. Today every row, input and button spans the whole window on a wide screen. PDR 036 sets a card's prompt and answers side by side on a wide screen.
  - Rejected: The phone's layout across the whole window, because the eye crosses the whole screen to read one row and a one-word value sits in an input as wide as the window. A column as wide as a phone's screen, because rows stay as cramped as on a phone though the room is there. A layout made for a wide screen, because it takes more work than publishing can wait for; its question is kept in the backlog.
  - Becomes true: A laptop user meets the layout they know from a phone, and nothing stretches across the window. The buttons of the bar at the foot are as wide as the form above them. A user who meets the app first on a laptop reads it as a phone's app. A table of a deck's cards and pasted rows are offered on no screen.
  - Drawings: 1
- PD2. On a run's row, the practice's name carries no mark. The row's mark is the run's, and the practice's name says which practice the run is of.
  - Facts: Home lists runs, never practices. Each row leads with the run's mark and day, and the practice's name sits close beside them. The run's mark is the practice's flash inside a circle, so the two marks are near alike. PDR 049 gives every practice's name its mark wherever the name appears, and rejected three other run marks as too near the plain flash at a phone's size. Home's run rows name the practice with no mark today.
  - Rejected: The practice mark beside the name, as PDR 049 has it, because two near-alike marks side by side blur which of the two the row is.
  - Becomes true: The user sees one mark on a run's row and reads the row as a run. The practice's name there is the one name with no mark, and the user tells its kind by where it sits.
- PD3. Anyone who opens the app can make an account and use it. No invitation is needed.
  - Facts: The app is published for people the owner gives its link to, and anyone else who finds it is welcome to use it. No one has used the app to study yet. PDR 012 keeps every deck to the user who made it.
  - Rejected: Accounts only for people who are invited, because a person handed the link would wait for an invitation before starting, and a stranger who found the app would meet a closed door.
  - Becomes true: A person given the link makes an account on their own and starts at once, and so does a stranger who finds the address.
- PD4. A visitor who is not logged in uses the app as a new user does, with no account. They land on the address they opened, Home shows the steps to a first practice, and everything a logged-in user can do is open to them. No page says what Flashy is. "Log in" stays in the top bar for anyone who wants an account.
  - Facts: The app is published for people given its link, and anyone who finds it is welcome. Some visitors come only to look, a recruiter among them. A visitor asked for an account before seeing anything is likely to leave, and may distrust typing an email and a password into a site they do not know. Today a visitor who is not logged in is given only "Log in".
  - Rejected: A page that says what Flashy is, with a way to try it and a way in, because it puts a page and a sentence between the visitor and the app. An account before anything is shown, because a visitor who came to look leaves having seen nothing. A welcome page of pictures, because the visitor sees the app but cannot use it, and the pictures go out of date. Example decks in the library of a visitor who is not logged in, because their Home would differ from a new user's.
  - Becomes true: A visitor is inside the working app the moment the page opens, with nothing to read or press first, and meets exactly what a new user with an account meets. A visitor who came only to look meets an empty app, and makes a deck, a card and a practice before they see a card practiced. A user who logs out lands in an empty app.
- PD5. The work of a guest, a visitor who is not logged in, lasts until they close or refresh the tab, and is then gone.
  - Facts: A guest uses the app with no account. The owner wants nothing of a guest's use to outlive the page. A phone can reload a tab on its own, a user refreshes a page that looks stuck, and PDR 031 has the user who meets a page that broke reload it in the browser.
  - Rejected: Work kept through a refresh and gone when the tab closes, and work kept in the browser until the guest clears it, because in both something of a guest's use outlives the page; kept in the browser, a guest never needs an account, and the next person at a shared computer finds the last guest's decks.
  - Becomes true: Nothing a guest makes outlives the page. A guest who refreshes, whose phone reloads the tab, or who reloads a page that broke loses their work.
- PD6. When a guest makes an account, everything they made as a guest goes into it. When a guest logs in to an account that already exists, the guest's work is dropped and that account's library is shown.
  - Facts: Under PD5 a guest loses their work on any refresh, so making an account is the one way to keep it. PDR 033 keeps a deck's name unique among its user's decks.
  - Rejected: Guest work always dropped, because the guest who made a deck and then decided to keep it would lose it at the moment they commit. Guest work always added to the account, because a guest's deck can share a name with one the account has, and practice tried as a guest would mix into the account's mastery.
  - Becomes true: A guest who makes an account finds everything they made already in it. A user who logs in to an account they already have gets that account's library, and what they made as a guest is gone.
- PD7. While a visitor is a guest, a banner in ink across the top of every page, in the form of the connection banner, says they are a guest and that logging in keeps their work. It is one line at the default text size. When the connection banner also shows, it sits above the guest's. Once a guest has made something, the browser asks before the tab is closed or refreshed.
  - Facts: A guest's work is gone when the tab closes or refreshes, and making an account through "Log in" keeps it. PDR 015 gives yellow to the one main action a screen is for, and ink to what is said. "Save" is the word for finishing a form. "Log in" sits in the top bar directly under the banner and opens the way to make an account. The banner's words fit one line on a phone at the default text size.
  - Rejected: Telling the guest nothing, or once when they first make something, because a guest who loses their work concludes the app lost it. A quiet grey line, because it is easy to miss. The browser's question alone, because it comes only at the moment of leaving, in the browser's words, and not when a phone reloads the tab. A yellow banner, because a form would then show three yellow things. Words saying the work is not saved, because they sit above a form's "Save". A button to make an account in the banner, because "Log in" already opens that way. Words telling the guest to make an account, because they leave the guest to find that "Log in" is the way to do it.
  - Becomes true: A guest reads on every page who they are and how to keep what they make, and gets a last chance before a close or refresh they caused. A guest gives up one line of the screen on every page. The banner's words are untrue for a guest who logs in to an account that already exists, and PD8 tells that guest so.
  - Drawings: 1, 2
- PD8. When a guest who has made something logs in to an account that already exists, the app asks before the guest's work is dropped. Cancel leaves them a guest, with their work still there. Discard takes them into their account.
  - Facts: Under PD6, logging in to an account that already exists drops a guest's work, and PD7's banner says logging in keeps it. The app asks before a user leaves a form with unsaved changes, with "Cancel" and "Discard".
  - Rejected: A warning before the log-in dialog opens for every guest with work, because the newcomer making a first account loses nothing and must read and dismiss it. No warning, because the banner's promise is then broken silently. Adding the guest's work to the existing account, because PD6 rejects it.
  - Becomes true: Only the user whose guest work is about to be dropped is asked, at the moment it applies, and keeps it by backing out. A newcomer goes from the log-in dialog straight into their new account with their work in it.
- PD9. "Log in" is a yellow block with the ink outline and shadow, on every page a guest sees.
  - Facts: Only a guest sees "Log in", and a guest uses every page. The guest's banner tells the guest to log in. PDR 015 allows one yellow thing on a screen besides the logo, and PDR 020 draws everything that can be tapped as a block. Today "Log in" is yellow with no outline and no shadow.
  - Rejected: A white block, because it stands out less than yellow. Yellow only on pages with no main action of their own, because the same button would change color from page to page.
  - Becomes true: A guest finds "Log in" as the brightest thing in the top bar on every page, drawn as a block like everything else that can be tapped. On a page with a main action of its own, two things are yellow, and the guest tells the page's main action by where it sits.
  - Drawings: 1, 2
- PD10. The steps to a first practice stay on Home until the user has started a run, with a fourth step, "Start run", that starts a run of the practice the user made and opens its first card. When the user has made several practices and run none, the step starts the one made most recently.
  - Facts: PD4 starts every visitor as a new user. PDR 048 shows the three steps until the user has made a practice, then lists runs. A user who has made a practice and started no run has no run to list, so Home shows its heading and the way to All practices and nothing between. A practice just made opens on its own page, where "Start run" stands.
  - Rejected: Home as it is, because the user one press from their first card finds a blank page, and must know to open All practices and then the practice. A sentence saying there are no runs yet, because it tells the user nothing to do.
  - Becomes true: A user who has made a practice finds on Home one press that puts them in front of their first card. The steps read four, and leave Home once a run is started.
- PD11. The search is taken out of the top bar until it finds something.
  - Facts: The search sits in the top bar on every page, takes typing, and does nothing when the user presses Enter. Once the app is published, every visitor meets it. What a search finds and where its results appear is undecided.
  - Rejected: Leaving it, because a control that takes typing and then does nothing reads as broken. Deciding what it finds and building it before publishing, because it pushes the launch back.
  - Becomes true: Nothing in the top bar leads nowhere. Every page on a phone gains the search's row back. The user has no search.
  - Drawings: 1, 2
