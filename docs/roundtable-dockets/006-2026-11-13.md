## Product decisions

- PD1 No page shows a "<". The row above a page names only the places the page belongs to, and a page that belongs to no place has no row. The user goes back to the page they came from with their browser's or phone's back.
  - Facts: A person may use Flashy in a phone's browser, from a home-screen icon with no browser bar, or on a wide screen. The "<" does what the browser's back does. The browser's back returns the user to the page they came from, as they left it.
  - Rejected: A "<" leading the row whenever there is an earlier page in Flashy, as PDR 017 decides, because it repeats the browser's back. A "<" only when Flashy is opened from a home-screen icon, because the top of a page would then differ by how Flashy was opened. A "<" only on About and Privacy, because the "<" would then show on some pages and not others.
  - Becomes true: The top of a page shows only where it sits. A user who opens Flashy from an iPhone's home-screen icon has no way back to the page they came from, and leaves About and Privacy by the menu.
  - Drawings: 1, 6, 8, 11

## Lesser decisions

- LPD1 The page that says what Flashy is is reached by "About" at the side menu's foot, set apart below the main places, and headed "About Flashy". It holds, in order, Flashy's one-line description; its three ideas, each a heading over one line; and "How it works" over four numbered steps, from deck and card to practice, run and summary. It draws no block and nothing yellow, and a thin line parts the ideas from the steps. Its words are VC1's and VC2's.
  - Facts: Nobody reads long paragraphs. Nothing on the page can be tapped, and the page has no main action. In Flashy's own menu, "About" can mean only Flashy.
  - Rejected: A strip at the foot of every page, because it collides with a form's bar and crowds the run page. Long paragraphs of explanation, because few finish them. A second line under the heading, because it is too wordy. The logo's yellow F in the heading, yellow discs behind the ideas' icons, a wavy yellow underline under each idea's heading and a yellow marker stroke through each, because the plain page is preferred and every one but the F gives yellow a meaning beside the main action. "About Flashy" in the menu, because "About" says it beside "Privacy". "About" as the heading, because someone arriving straight at the page reads nothing saying what it is about.
  - Becomes true: The user reads what Flashy is and how to use it on one phone screen, one tap on the menu away.
  - Drawings: 8
- LPD2 Deleting an account is "Delete account", below "Log out" in the account sheet. It opens a confirm headed "Delete your account?" over "Your decks, cards, practices and runs will be gone, with your mastery. This can't be undone.", with "Cancel" and "Delete".
  - Facts: Every deletion in Flashy confirms with the thing's name and "This can't be undone."
  - Rejected: Typing "DELETE" to confirm, because no other deletion asks it and it slows a user who has decided. "Delete your account? This can't be undone." alone, because the user is not told their mastery goes.
  - Becomes true: A user reads exactly what they lose before deleting their account.
  - Drawings: 10
- LPD3 The privacy page is reached by "Privacy" in the side menu, directly below "About", and headed "Privacy". Under its heading it reads VC8's opening line, then six headings, each over its words, as VC8 gives them. The address to write to is underlined inside its sentence and opens the person's email app.
  - Facts: Google's review asks a privacy policy to say what is collected, who receives it, how it is deleted and whom to contact.
  - Rejected: The address as a block, because a block for one address at the foot of a page of text is out of place. The address as plain text, because the user copies it by hand.
  - Becomes true: A person reads, in under a minute, what Flashy keeps about them, and writes to Flashy's owner in one tap.
  - Drawings: 9, 11
