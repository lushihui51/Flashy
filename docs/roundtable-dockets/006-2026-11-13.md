## Open questions

- Q1. How is Flashy hosted and deployed: the provider for the API and for the frontend, whether they share an origin, the managed PostgreSQL, Clerk's production keys, where `alembic upgrade` runs on a deploy, and where secrets live?
  - Reason: Nothing in the repository deploys; `.github/workflows/ci.yml` runs tests and the migration chain only (ADR 041). Four things wait on the answer: mapping a database outage to 503 on every endpoint once the frontend and API are cross-origin (ADR 072); removing the local auth bypass in `app/dependencies.py` once local testing no longer needs it; rate limiting of `GET /api/health` (ADR 072); and a startup check that the database is at alembic head (ADR 045).
  - Owner: /code-decide
  - Needed by: nothing
- Q2. Do the practice's vocabulary row and the run's rows give their code names, `practice`, `Practice` and `/practices` for a practice and `RunStatus` and `status` for a run's state?
  - Reason: the row "A named set of decks the user keeps and runs as many times as they like" reads "—" in Code, though the `practice` table and the `Practice` model name its concept; no row's Code names `RunStatus` or `RunRead.status`, and "A run that can still be continued" and "A run that has finished" give only `active` and `completed`.
  - Owner: /code-decide
  - Needed by: nothing
- Q3. Is a rule or a piece of a screen that several places write alike kept in one shared place, and which of today's repeats move there?
  - Reason: no record says when a repeat earns one home. Two stand today. The rule for a name in a list, two lines then "…" with a word wider than its line broken inside itself, is written as the classes `line-clamp-2` and `break-words` at 14 places in 11 files under `frontend/src/components` and `frontend/src/pages`, so a new list must remember both halves. A heading never narrower than its mark and longest word, whose circle and buttons wrap beneath it, is written three times, in `DeckDetailPage` in `frontend/src/pages/DeckDetailPage.tsx`, `PracticeDetailsPage` in `frontend/src/pages/PracticeDetailsPage.tsx` and `CardSheetContent` in `frontend/src/components/run/RunBreakdown.tsx`, where a `min-w-0` added to any copy brings back the break inside a word; eleven pages also repeat the heading's text classes.
  - Owner: /code-decide
  - Needed by: nothing
- Q4. Which test cases guard nothing a change could break, and are they removed?
  - Reason: no record says what a test case must guard to be kept. The frontend holds 732 cases in 76 files and the backend 384. Some assert a class name, as "rows with and without a subtitle share the same min-height class" in `frontend/src/components/ui/ListRow.test.tsx` and the `h-10` of "Add deck" in `frontend/src/pages/LibraryPage.test.tsx`, which restates the source, since jsdom does no layout (ADR 017); "keys by whatever the caller passes as key" in `ListRow.test.tsx` renders a list and asserts its links' addresses, which the cases beside it already assert. Which kinds of case go, and whether anything replaces one that goes, is undecided.
  - Owner: /code-decide
  - Needed by: nothing
- Q5. How is a guest's use held with no account, so that it works as a logged-in user's does, is gone when the tab closes or refreshes, goes into a new account when the guest makes one, and is dropped after the confirm when they log in to an account that already exists; and how does the app tell a new account from an existing one at that moment?
  - Reason: PDR 053 and PD7 and PD9 to PD19 decide what a guest meets, and today every part of the app needs a logged-in user. CD1 answers where a guest's work is held, and every other part waits on Q8.
  - Owner: /code-decide
  - Needed by: nothing
- Q8. How is the example library made, kept the same for every visitor and shown to a visitor who is not logged in, with nothing a visitor does changing it; and do Q5 and CD1, which hold a guest's own work, still stand now that a guest makes none?
  - Reason: PD9 gives a visitor who is not logged in one example library to look through and nothing to change, and PD10 and PD16 to PD19 say what it holds. Today every part of the app needs a logged-in user. Q5 and CD1 were written for a guest who makes decks and practices.

## Product decisions

- PD7. While a visitor is a guest, a banner in ink across the top of every page, in the form of the connection banner, says that what they see is an example and that logging in opens all of Flashy. It is one line at the default text size. When the connection banner also shows, it sits above the guest's.
  - Facts: Under PD9 a guest looks through an example library they did not make, on pages that read as a user's own, "Your library" among them. PDR 054 gives yellow to the one main action a screen is for, and ink to what is said. "Log in" sits in the top bar directly under the banner. The banner's words fit one line on a phone at the default text size.
  - Rejected: Telling the guest nothing, because they find decks and runs they never made, with nothing saying whose they are. A quiet grey line, because it is easy to miss. A yellow banner, because a page with a main action of its own would then show three yellow things. A button to log in in the banner, because "Log in" already sits directly under it. Saying only that it is an example, because a guest who presses nothing is never told what logging in gives.
  - Becomes true: A guest reads on every page that what they see is an example, and how to get past it. A guest gives up one line of the screen on every page. A guest whose screen has dimmed reads the same words twice, in the banner and beneath "Log in".
  - Drawings: 1, 2, 3, 4
- PD9. A visitor who is not logged in is shown one example library, the same for every visitor, to look through. They can change nothing: whatever would make, change or delete something, or rate a card, tells them to log in instead.
  - Facts: Some visitors come only to look, a recruiter among them. A visitor asked for an account before seeing anything is likely to leave. The owner wants a visitor who is not logged in only to see how the app runs. Rating a card changes something. PDR 053 lets such a visitor use the app as a new user does. PDR 012 shows on no screen a deck made by someone else.
  - Rejected: The whole app, empty, as a new user meets it, because it is too much to build for what it gives, the visitor makes a deck, a card and a practice before seeing a card practiced, and loses them on a refresh. An example library the visitor can also practice, because the owner wants a visitor only to see how the app runs. Nothing but "Log in", because the visitor who came to look leaves having seen nothing.
  - Becomes true: A visitor sees a filled app the moment the page opens. A visitor makes nothing, so nothing of theirs can be lost. No one makes a deck or practices a card before they have an account.
- PD10. The example library holds what a user who has practiced for a while has: decks with their cards, practices of those decks, completed runs, and runs left partway. Every page of the app has something to show.
  - Facts: Under PD9 a visitor who is not logged in starts no run and makes nothing, so they see only what the example library already holds. The owner wants such a visitor to see how the app runs.
  - Rejected: No run left partway, because the visitor has no way to reach the screen where a card is practiced. Practices never run, because every mastery circle is empty and the visitor sees no card practiced and no summary. Decks and cards only, because the visitor learns nothing of practices, runs or mastery.
  - Becomes true: A visitor finds something on every kind of page the app has, a card in a run and a run's summary included. A visitor who makes an account goes from a filled app to an empty one.
- PD11. A guest opens every form and reads it. What the form holds is greyed and cannot be changed, and its foot holds Done alone.
  - Facts: Under PD9 a guest changes nothing. Most acts that change something begin by opening a form. PDR 051 puts Done alone at the foot of a form that only shows what cannot be changed, and a deck's board in a practice reads that way while a run is active.
  - Rejected: No form opening, because every "Add", every pencil and every row of the top bar's New answers with the same sentence and the guest never sees how a thing is made. Forms that work until Create, Save or Delete, because the guest learns after typing that none of it is kept. Only the form of a thing that exists opening, because the same kind of press then does two different things.
  - Becomes true: A guest sees what making each thing asks of them, and types nothing that could be lost. A guest meets an empty, greyed form behind every "New" and "Add". A guest cannot try moving a field on a board.
  - Drawings: 1, 2, 3, 4
- PD12. When a guest presses something that would change the example library, a greyed form's inputs included, the whole screen dims but for "Log in", and a sentence in white beneath "Log in" tells them to log in. A tap anywhere else brings the screen back as it was, and nothing has changed.
  - Facts: Under PD9 every act that would change something tells a guest to log in, and such acts sit on many surfaces: "Start run", a completed run's play button, "Re-run", "Re-run these", "End run", a card's ratings and every form. "Log in" is in the top bar of every page a guest sees, in yellow. While a picker's list is open the page is dimmed, and a tap on the dimmed page closes the list and acts on nothing. Most guests come to look. PDR 031 lets nothing pop up over the page for a failure. The connection banner's words are white on red, and the guest's banner is drawn in white on ink.
  - Rejected: A sentence at each control, because it shows in a different place on every surface, and every new control needs a place of its own. "Log in" as the only thing that can be pressed, because a guest who pressed out of curiosity is shut out of the example until they log in or reload. Those controls greyed with the reason in the banner alone, because the reason sits a screen away and the app looks switched off. The press opening the log-in dialog, because the guest is asked for an email with no word of why. A message that pops up, because it sits away from what was pressed and is gone in seconds. The sentence on an ink block, because the owner wants the words white straight against the dimmed screen.
  - Becomes true: A guest meets one answer to every press that would change something, at the thing to press next. A guest taps once more to get back to the page. A guest reads the reason at the top of the screen, away from the control they pressed. A form shows a guest no sentence of its own. A guest reads the sentence as small white words over a dimmed page that shows through, at about 3.5 to 1, less than small text needs.
  - Drawings: 3, 4
- PD13. Home shows a guest the example library's runs as it shows a user their own: the runs left partway under Active, the most recent one's play button Home's main action, and the completed runs under Recently completed. A guest never sees the steps to a first practice.
  - Facts: Under PD10 the example library holds completed runs and runs left partway. PDR 057 lists a user's runs on Home once they have started one, and takes the steps off Home for good. PDR 053 has a visitor's Home show the steps.
  - Rejected: The steps to a first practice, because they count what the user has made and the example library holds all of it. A Home of its own saying what Flashy is, because it puts a page and a sentence between the visitor and the app, and the banner carries that sentence.
  - Becomes true: A guest reaches a card in one press from Home. A guest who presses a completed run's play button meets the dimmed screen. A user first meets the steps after logging in to a new account.
- PD14. A visitor who logs in lands on Home, in their account, whatever page they were on. A user who logs out lands on Home, in the example library.
  - Facts: A guest presses "Log in" on whatever page they are on, often a page of an example deck, practice or run, and their account holds none of those. PDR 012 and PDR 031 answer the address of a thing the user does not have with "<Thing> not found." PDR 053 has a visitor land on the address they opened.
  - Rejected: The same address after logging in, because a guest who logs in from an example deck's page reads "Deck not found." as the first screen of their new account. The same page where the account has it and Home otherwise, because the landing would then differ by the page the visitor was on.
  - Becomes true: Every log-in and every log-out lands on Home. A new user's first screen is Home with the steps to a first practice. A returning user who opened an address of their own while logged out opens it again after logging in.
- PD15. On a run's card, a guest opens an answer's "Rate" list and reads its four ratings. Choosing one dims the screen.
  - Facts: A user rates an answer by pressing "Rate" and choosing Again, Hard, Good or Easy from its list, and moves to the next card once every answer is rated. Under PD9 rating tells a guest to log in. "Show answer" changes nothing in the example library.
  - Rejected: "Rate" itself dimming the screen, because the guest never reads the four ratings. Ratings that can be chosen, with the move to the next card dimming the screen, because the guest learns after choosing that it does not count.
  - Becomes true: A guest reads everything practicing a card asks of them, up to the choice. A guest never sees a rated answer, or the card after.
- PD16. The example library's runs show the days they were really run.
  - Facts: A run is named by the day it was run, written as a date, on Home, on a practice's page and as the heading of a completed run's page. The example library is the same for every visitor, so its runs were run before the app was published.
  - Rejected: Days that keep pace with the visitor's today, because the owner finds it more than a guest's look at the app is worth.
  - Becomes true: A guest reads the same dates on the example's runs whenever they visit. A guest who visits months after the app is published reads runs dated months back.
- PD17. The example library holds a deck named "Common words", of about fifty everyday words, each card holding the word in five fields in this order: English, Chinese, Korean, French, Spanish.
  - Facts: The owner's own decks stay out of the example library. The owner wants material anybody knows. PDR 006 names a card, wherever it is listed, by the value of its deck's first field. The app's own words are English.
  - Rejected: Chinese first, because a guest who does not read Chinese cannot tell one card's row from the next. The owner's own decks, alone or beside this one, because they stay the owner's.
  - Becomes true: A guest reads every card's name in English. A guest sees a card that holds five fields. A guest who speaks one of the languages notices a wrong translation.
  - Drawings: 1, 2
- PD18. The example library holds a second, smaller deck named "Capitals", of twenty well-known countries, each card holding three fields in this order: Country, Capital, Continent.
  - Facts: The owner wants a second, smaller deck, on something other than language, that anybody knows. PDR 035 makes a practice a named set of decks.
  - Rejected: "Common words" alone, because no practice could then hold more than one deck and the guest never sees decks gathered. A second language deck, because the owner wants something other than language. The eight planets, because eight cards give a run little to show. The first twenty elements, because a guest who has forgotten school chemistry cannot judge an answer.
  - Becomes true: A guest sees two decks with different fields in Your library, and a practice can hold both. A guest who knows no second language still finds answers they can judge.
  - Drawings: 1, 2
- PD19. The example library holds four practices, each with completed runs. "Common words + Capitals" holds both decks: a Common words card shows English and asks Spanish, and a Capitals card shows Country and asks Capital and Continent. "Korean from Chinese and English" holds Common words: every card shows Chinese and English and asks Korean. "Two at random" holds Common words: every card shows English and asks two of the other four languages, drawn at random each time the card comes up. "French from English" holds Common words: every card shows English and asks French. Three have a run left partway: "Common words + Capitals", the most recent, at a Capitals card on its first attempt, with no card failed so far; "Korean from Chinese and English", at a card that has come back as a retry, with others still to come back; and "Two at random", at a card on its first attempt, with some cards retried earlier in the run. "French from English" has none.
  - Facts: A guest cannot move a run on, so each run left partway is one card they see. PDR 038 gives a practice at most one active run. PDR 057 lists up to three active runs on Home, and under Recently completed no run of a practice that has an active one. The owner wants a practice that asks Korean from Chinese and English, a practice that shows random draw, and several runs left partway, some with retries and some without.
  - Rejected: One run left partway, because the guest sees one card. Two, because the guest sees one card fewer than Home's Active can hold. All four, because Home shows only three and nothing under Recently completed. A practice asking all four other languages, because the owner wants Korean asked from Chinese and English in its place. "Two at random" drawing its prompt at random too, because some cards would open on a prompt in Chinese or Korean alone. One practice of both decks alone, because the guest never sees a deck asked in different ways. One practice for each deck, because no practice gathers decks.
  - Becomes true: A guest sees three different cards, one from each run left partway, and the first from Home has two answers anyone can check. A guest sees one deck asked three ways on three boards. A guest finds three runs under Active on Home and one under Recently completed.
- PD20. On a wide screen, whatever opens over a page opens inside the column, as it does on a phone's screen: the menu from the column's left edge, a sheet from the foot at the column's width, and a question the app asks in the column's middle. The dimming behind any of them covers the whole window. A picture opened from a card is the one exception, and is shown as large as the window allows.
  - Facts: PDR 052 shows every page on a wide screen in one column in the middle of the window, holds the top bar's contents and a form's foot buttons to it, and says nothing of what opens over a page. Today the menu opens at the window's left edge, away from its button, and a sheet, a question and an opened picture span the window. A user opens a picture to see it larger.
  - Rejected: The picture held to the column too, because it stays small with an empty window on either side. Everything across the window, because a sheet's rows run the whole width and the menu opens far from its button. The menu always open down the left side, because it is a layout made for a wide screen, which waits until the app is published.
  - Becomes true: A user on a wide screen finds the menu beside its button and a sheet as wide as the page beneath it. A user sees an opened picture at the window's size. A guest on a wide screen reads PD12's sentence in the column while the whole window dims.
  - Drawings: 4

## Code decisions

- CD1. A guest's work is held in the database, as the rows of an `app_user` of its own that has no Clerk identity, read and written through the same routes, services and database operations as a logged-in user's.
  - Facts: Every route handler but `GET /api/health` takes `CurrentUserDep`. Ownership hangs from `app_user.id` on `deck`, `practice` and `review_log`. Mastery arithmetic, run generation, the deletion closure, Home's read and a run's day are worked out on the server (ADR 116, ADR 112, ADR 118, ADR 123, ADR 114). Clerk offers no user without credentials. Nothing of a guest's use outliving the page means nothing a person can find again, and a row may remain in the database after the tab closes.
  - Rejected: A TypeScript implementation answering the API in the browser, because every rule is written twice and drifts with no test failing, and making an account would import reviews stamped by the browser's clock, against ADR 019. The backend's Python run in the browser on a PostgreSQL built for the browser, because both download before the first page works. Tables or a schema apart from accounts' data, because every migration runs twice and moving a guest's work into an account is a copy with every id remapped. A guest as a Clerk user, because Clerk has none.
  - Becomes true: A guest's use works as a logged-in user's with no route, service or page changed. A new account takes a guest's work by a change of owner on rows already there. A guest's rows stay after the tab closes until something removes them. Anyone can make the server write rows with no account.
- CD2. `app_user.first_practice_at` is renamed `first_run_at`, an instant stamped by the server while it is null, inside `cut_run`'s transaction in `app/services/run.py`, and written by nothing else. `GET /api/home` returns `steps` as null once it is set. Until then `steps` carries `has_deck`, `has_card` and `practice_id`, the id of the user's most recently made practice or null. The third step is ticked when `practice_id` is set, and the fourth step calls `POST /api/practices/{practice_id}/runs` with it and opens the run. The migration renames the column, sets it to the instant of each user's earliest run, and leaves it null for a user with no run.
  - Facts: PDR 057 keeps Home's steps until the user has started a run and adds a fourth step that starts a run of the practice made most recently. `create_practice` in `app/services/practice.py` stamps `first_practice_at`, and `get_home` in `app/services/home.py` ends the steps on it (ADR 123). `HomeSteps` holds `has_deck` and `has_card` alone. `cut_run` cuts every run (ADR 112). A completed run's play button on Home already calls `startRun` and opens the run. Home's way to All practices shows only once the steps have left Home, as PDR 057 means it. The app is not published.
  - Rejected: The stamp at the first practice, because a user with a practice and no run sees no steps and no runs. A `first_run_at` beside `first_practice_at`, because the first is then written and read by nothing. The name kept and stamped at the first run, because it says practice and means run. No stamp, with the steps shown while the user has no run, because they come back after a deletion. A route of its own that picks the practice, because it is a route for one button. The client picking the practice from the list of practices, because it puts a selection rule in TypeScript and loads every practice.
  - Becomes true: The steps end at the first run cut on any path and never return. Home's read carries the practice its fourth step starts. A user in the dev database who ran a practice and then deleted it sees the steps again.
- CD3. The column's width is one `@theme` token in rem, `--container-column: 40rem`, in `frontend/src/index.css`, applied in the shell alone. `AppShell` holds the top bar's contents, each banner's text and `<main>` to it, centred, and `FormBar` holds its sentence, count and buttons to it less the page's side padding. The banners' and the foot bar's bands keep the window's width. No page writes the column, and no breakpoint is used.
  - Facts: PDR 052 shows every page on a wide screen in one column in the middle of the window, about one and a half times a phone's width, and holds the top bar's contents and the foot bar's buttons to it. Drawing 1 draws the header's contents and the page at 640px, the foot bar's buttons at 608px, and both bands across the window. Every page renders inside `AppShell`'s `<main>`, and every bar at the foot is `FormBar` (ADR 127). A design size used more than once is a `@theme` token in rem (ADR 101). No element caps its width today.
  - Rejected: Each page wrapping itself, because one of fifteen routes that forgets stretches across the window with nothing failing. A cap on the app's root, because an element fixed to the viewport ignores it. A breakpoint, because a cap does nothing below its own width. A value in px, because it holds still while the text grows.
  - Becomes true: A new page sits in the column with nothing to remember. A new element fixed to the viewport spans the window unless it applies the token, and no test sees it, since jsdom does no layout (ADR 017).
  - Drawings: 1
- CD4. `SearchBar` in `frontend/src/components/shell/SearchBar.tsx` and its test file are deleted, with its mount in `AppShell`.
  - Facts: LPD2 takes the search out of the top bar until it finds something. `SearchBar` has no user but `AppShell`, and Enter in it does nothing. What a search finds and where its results appear is undecided.
  - Rejected: The component kept and not mounted, because its tests would guard a control no screen shows.
  - Becomes true: No component exists for a control no screen shows.
  - Drawings: 1, 2

## Lesser decisions

- LPD1. "Log in" is drawn as a block, with the ink outline and shadow, on every page a guest sees.
  - Facts: PDR 020 draws everything that can be tapped as a block with an ink outline and shadow. PDR 054 makes "Log in" yellow on every page a guest sees. Today "Log in" is yellow with no outline and no shadow.
  - Rejected: None.
  - Becomes true: A guest finds "Log in" drawn as a block like everything else that can be tapped.
  - Drawings: 1, 2
- LPD2. The search is taken out of the top bar until it finds something.
  - Facts: The search sits in the top bar on every page, takes typing, and does nothing when the user presses Enter. Once the app is published, every visitor meets it. What a search finds and where its results appear is undecided.

  - Drawings: 1, 2
