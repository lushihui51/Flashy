## Open questions

- Q1 What should README.md hold so it stays true as Flashy changes?
  - Reason: README.md describes a summary with badges for first try, one retry, several retries and failed; says how a practice asks a deck has no screen, Home and search are placeholders and Flashy is not deployed; and says nothing of the example library.
  - Owner: /code-decide
  - Needed by: nothing

## Product decisions

- PD1 We will have a page that says what Flashy is. No one meets it before the app: a guest still lands on the address they opened, and a user who logs in or out still lands on Home. Anyone, guest or user, reaches it from every page by a link in the side menu, set apart at the menu's foot below the main places.
  - Facts: Google's sign-in review asks for a public home page that says what the app does. A user wants to look up what Flashy is when they choose, never before meeting the app.
  - Rejected: The account sheet, because a guest never sees it. The foot of Home, because it is reached from Home alone and missed below the runs. The guest's banner, because a logged-in user has none. A strip at the foot of every page, because it collides with a form's bar and crowds the run page. No such page, as PDR 053 decides, because Google's review needs one and a curious user has nowhere to look.
  - Becomes true: A guest or a user reads what Flashy is from any page, one tap on the menu away. PDR 053's decision to have no page that says what Flashy is no longer holds.
  - Drawings: 8
- PD2 We will have the page that says what Flashy is hold, in this order: Flashy's one-line description; the three ideas it is built on, that a card is a set of named fields, that what a card asks changes every time it appears, and that mastery belongs to each field; and how it works, from deck and card to practice, run and summary, as the app works today. Every word is one the app shows. It holds nothing about how Flashy is built or run.
  - Facts: README.md holds Flashy's description, its three ideas and how it works, and parts of it no longer match the app.
  - Rejected: Adding the comparison with Anki, because a user who does not know Anki reads about another product. Adding how Flashy is built, because it is not about the user. The description alone, because the user would not learn what a practice or a run is. The whole README, because the user meets set-up steps and tables they cannot use.
  - Becomes true: The user reads what Flashy is, why it works as it does, and how to use it, in the screens' own words.
- PD3 We will name the side menu's link to the page that says what Flashy is "About", and the page's heading "About Flashy". Under the heading the page reads "Flashcards for knowledge that doesn't fit on two sides of a card."; then three ideas, each a heading over one line: "One card, many fields" over "A word's spelling, meaning, gender and example, all on one card."; "A new question every time" over "Choose what each card shows and asks. Fields drawn at random keep the same card fresh."; "Mastery for every field" over "See exactly what you know. The fields you know least come up most."; then "How it works" over four numbered steps: "Make a deck and fill it with cards.", "Make a practice from your decks, and choose what each one asks.", "Start a run. Rate each answer, and missed cards come back." and "See how each card's mastery moved, then run it again." The page draws no block and nothing yellow, and a thin line parts the ideas from the steps.
  - Facts: Nobody reads long paragraphs. Nothing on the page can be tapped, and the page has no main action. In Flashy's own menu, "About" can mean only Flashy.
  - Rejected: Long paragraphs of explanation, because few finish them. A second line under the heading, because it is too wordy. The logo's yellow F in the heading, yellow discs behind the ideas' icons, a wavy yellow underline under each idea's heading and a yellow marker stroke through each, because the plain page is preferred and every one but the F gives yellow a meaning beside the main action. "About Flashy" in the menu, because "About" says it beside "Privacy". "About" as the heading, because someone arriving straight at the page reads nothing saying what it is about.
  - Becomes true: The user reads what Flashy is and how to use it on one phone screen.
  - Drawings: 8
- PD4 We will let a user delete their account from the account sheet, after a confirm that says it cannot be undone. Their account, their sign-in and everything they made in Flashy go at once, and they land on Home in the example library, as after logging out.
  - Facts: Google's review expects the privacy page to say how a person gets their data deleted. No one can delete their account in Flashy. Deleting an account at the sign-in service leaves everything Flashy holds about it.
  - Rejected: An email to the contact address, with the deletion done by hand, because the user writes, waits and must trust it happened. Both the button and the email, because the button alone is chosen.
  - Becomes true: A user deletes their account and everything in it themselves, at any time.
  - Drawings: 10
- PD5 We will show "Delete account" below "Log out" in the account sheet. It opens a confirm headed "Delete your account?" over "Your decks, cards, practices and runs will be gone, with your mastery. This can't be undone.", with "Cancel" and "Delete".
  - Facts: Every deletion in Flashy confirms with the thing's name and "This can't be undone."
  - Rejected: Typing "DELETE" to confirm, because no other deletion asks it and it slows a user who has decided. "Delete your account? This can't be undone." alone, because the user is not told their mastery goes.
  - Becomes true: A user reads exactly what they lose before deleting their account.
  - Drawings: 10
- PD6 We will reach the privacy page from a link reading "Privacy" in the side menu, directly below "About", from every page, for guest and user alike. Its heading reads "Privacy".
  - Facts: Google's review requires a public privacy policy reachable from the app's home page.
  - Rejected: A link at the foot of the page that says what Flashy is, because the menu is preferred. The account sheet, because a guest never sees it, Google's reviewer among them.
  - Becomes true: Anyone reaches the privacy page from any page, one tap on the menu away.
  - Drawings: 8, 9
- PD7 We will have the privacy page read, under its heading, "Flashy is run by Jaden Lu. This page says what Flashy keeps about you, and why."; then each heading over its words: "Looking around" over "If you look around without logging in, Flashy keeps nothing about you."; "Signing in" over "You sign in through Clerk, a sign-in service. When you sign in with Google, Clerk receives your name, email address and profile picture. Flashy shows them in your account and uses them for nothing else."; "What Flashy keeps" over "What you make and do in Flashy: your decks, cards, practices and runs, every rating you give, and when you started your first run. And your time zone, so dates show in your own time."; "Who else has it" over "Flashy sells nothing, shares nothing and has no ads or tracking. Clerk holds your sign-in, and everything else is stored on Railway, the service that runs Flashy's server."; "Deleting it" over ""Delete account", in your account, deletes your account and everything in it at once. It can't be undone."; and "Questions" over "Write to shihui.lu51@gmail.com.". The address is an underlined link inside its sentence that opens the person's email app, the one thing in Flashy that can be tapped and is not drawn as a block.
  - Facts: Google's review asks a privacy policy to say what is collected, who receives it, how it is deleted and whom to contact. Flashy keeps, for each user, the sign-in service's name for them, their time zone, when they started their first run, and everything they make and rate. A person's name, email address and picture from Google stay with Clerk, and Flashy shows them only in the account sheet. A guest changes nothing and Flashy keeps nothing of them. Flashy has no ads, analytics or tracking. Jaden Lu runs Flashy and answers at shihui.lu51@gmail.com.
  - Rejected: A standard policy in legal language, because users do not read it and it claims more than the facts. Leaving out Clerk and Railway, because Google asks who receives the data and the user could not find where their data lives. The address as a block, as PDR 020 draws everything that can be tapped, because a block for one address at the foot of a page of text is out of place. The address as plain text, because the user copies it by hand.
  - Becomes true: A person reads, in under a minute, what Flashy keeps about them, who else holds it, and how to delete it. PDR 020 holds everywhere but this address.
  - Drawings: 11
