# Roundtable docket 005: 2026-11-07

Canvas: https://claude.ai/artifact/HK6azs7VhTjzRJnzetbg8Q

## Open questions

- Q10. How does a form leave so that, once the user has left it by Cancel, Create or Save, the "<" on the page they land on goes back past the form, as PDR 017 means, without Cancel relying on the browser's history, which ADR 103 rejected?
  - Reason: on the run page, opening Edit card and tapping Cancel returns to the run page, and its "<" then opens Edit card rather than the page the user reached the run page from. PDR 017 has the "<" do what the browser's back does; PDR 019 returns the user from the form and does not mention the "<". Every form leaves by a new history entry and the "<" steps back one, so the form stays one step behind.
  - Owner: /code-decide
  - Needed by: nothing

## Product decisions

- PD1. Home has two sections, "Active" and "Recently completed". Active lists up to three active practices, the most recent first. Recently completed lists the three most recently completed practices, the newest first. Every row in either section is a practice: its name, its mastery and a round play button, a triangle inside a circle. The play button opens the practice's next card, with no page between. Tapping the row elsewhere opens its details beneath it, with "Hide", which closes them.
  - Facts:
    - The user reading "Recently practiced" expects practices and finds decks.
    - A row on Home and its "Start practice" both reach the practice, so "Start practice" reads as useless.
    - Only the most recent active practice is one tap from its next card.
  - Rejected:
    - PDR 016's Recently practiced list of decks: the user looks for the practice they did and finds decks, and a deck also in an active practice shows in both lists.
    - A row that opens the practice's page, with "Start practice" there: two taps to the next card, and a page between that the user does not need.
  - Becomes true: every practice on Home is one tap from its next card, and both sections list practices. This supersedes PDR 016's lists.
  - Drawings: 2
- PD2. A practice's details on Home list each of its decks by deck. An active practice's details add how many of its cards are passed, failed, retrying and unseen. A completed practice's details add "N cards practiced" and the five change counts its summary shows.
  - Facts:
    - The row alone names the practice, not what it holds.
    - PD1 opens details from the row.
  - Rejected:
    - The decks alone: the user cannot see how far an active practice has gone, or what a completed one did.
    - Details holding "Open practice" and "Delete practice": the play button already starts it, and deleting from Home puts a lasting act beside a passing glance.
  - Becomes true: the user can see what a practice holds and how it went without leaving Home.
  - Drawings: 2
- PD3. A practice is renamed on Edit practice, opened by a pencil beside the practice page's main action, as a subject's and a deck's pages open theirs. Edit practice holds the practice's name and "Delete practice"; the practice's page no longer offers Delete itself. Renaming leaves the practice where it was in every list, which stays ordered by when it was made.
  - Facts:
    - A practice's name is typed on New practice or set to the day it was made, and nothing changes it afterwards.
    - A subject and a deck are renamed and deleted from their own Edit page, opened by a pencil beside the page's main action.
  - Rejected:
    - Renaming in place on the practice's page: unlike every other thing the user renames.
    - Keeping Delete on the practice's page beside Edit practice: two ways to one lasting act, unlike a subject and a deck.
    - No renaming: two practices made the same day stay told apart only by a number.
  - Becomes true: the user names a practice as they name a subject or a deck. This extends PDR 029.
  - Drawings: 4
- PD4. Home's main action, in yellow, is the play button of the most recent active practice. With no active practice, it is the play button of the most recently completed practice.
  - Facts:
    - PDR 016 makes starting the most recent active practice Home's main action.
    - PD1 puts a play button on every row of both sections.
  - Rejected:
    - Every play button in yellow: no one action stands out.
    - No main action with no active practice: Home offers nothing to go on with.
  - Becomes true: Home always has one main action while the user has any practice.
  - Drawings: 2
- PD5. The play button on a completed practice's row starts a new practice of the same decks, each with the configuration it was practiced with, and opens its first card without asking. The completed practice stays as it was.
  - Facts:
    - PD1 has every row's play button go straight to a card, and a completed practice has no card left.
    - Re-run makes a new practice of the same decks after "Re-run this practice?".
  - Rejected:
    - Asking "Re-run this practice?" first: the play button promises a card, and the completed practice is kept, so nothing is lost by not asking.
    - Opening the completed practice's summary: the play button would mean two things on one screen.
  - Becomes true: the user practices the same decks again in one tap from Home.
- PD6. On the summary, a kind of change that no card had shows its change badge in its own color and its zero with no block around them, and cannot be chosen. Only a count above zero, drawn as a block, narrows the list.
  - Facts:
    - A count of zero can be chosen and narrows the list to nothing.
    - A greyed badge hides whether it is red or ink.
  - Rejected:
    - Greying the badge and its zero: the user cannot tell red from ink.
    - A dashed outline around a zero: it still reads as something to tap.
    - Hiding a zero: the user cannot tell whether the kind of change exists.
    - A greyed block around a zero: it still reads as something to tap.
  - Becomes true: the user never narrows the summary to an empty list, and every badge keeps its color.
  - Drawings: 5
- PD7. Every form backs out to the page that opened it, wherever it was opened from: any page, Home's first-practice steps or the top bar's New sheet. After Create, a form lands on what it made.
  - Facts:
    - Cancel on New practice opened from Home lands on the Practice page, not on Home.
    - Home's "New practice", its steps and the top bar's New sheet open forms that back out to their thing's list.
    - PDR 017 has a form back out to whatever opened it.
  - Rejected:
    - Backing out to the thing's list: the user lands somewhere they did not come from.
  - Becomes true: Cancel always returns the user to where they were.
- PD8. The summary keeps its chosen order and counts as a page keeps its tab and filters: across a refresh, browser back and its address. Reached fresh from a list, it opens on every card, ordered by mastery.
  - Facts:
    - PDR 017 keeps a page's open tab and filters across browser back.
    - PDR 024 gives the summary three orders and counts the user can choose.
  - Rejected:
    - Forgetting the chosen order and counts on leaving: the user who opens a card and comes back loses what they narrowed to.
  - Becomes true: the user who opens a card from the summary and comes back finds the list as they left it.
  - Drawings: 6
- PD9. Every date is written as "2026-11-03", with no time. New practice's starting name is the day's date; a further practice made the same day adds, after a space, the next untaken number in parentheses, as "2026-11-03 (1)". Existing names are kept.
  - Facts:
    - The app writes a date and time three ways: "Nov 3, 2026, 10:42 p.m.", "Oct 1, 2026 at 1:18 PM" and "3 Nov 2026, 22:42".
    - A time in a name gives the user more numbers than they need to tell practices apart.
  - Rejected:
    - The date with the time: too many numbers for what the user reads.
    - A written month, as "Nov 3, 2026": longer, and it orders differently from how it reads.
    - Renaming existing practices: names the user may have typed would change under them.
  - Becomes true: every date in the app reads one way, and two practices of one day are told apart by a short number.
  - Drawings: 2, 4, 7
- PD10. When a row's contents are wider than the screen, the row wraps them onto further lines, and nothing on a page scrolls sideways. A name or heading breaks only between words. In a list, a title wraps to at most two lines and is shortened after that; on its own page, a name's heading is never shortened. An input too narrow to type in moves beneath its label and takes the full width.
  - Facts:
    - PDR 030 has everything grow with the browser's text size.
    - At the largest text size on a phone, the top bar's "Log in", the Practice page's "Completed" tab and a deck's "Add configuration" run past the edge, and the whole page scrolls sideways.
    - A deck's heading breaks inside its word, Edit deck's field name inputs narrow to about one character, the Practice page's filters cut their words to "All subj" and "All deck", and a row's title is shortened to a few characters, so several rows look alike.
  - Rejected:
    - Each row scrolling sideways on its own: what is past the edge stays unseen until the user swipes.
    - Shrinking what the row holds: it undoes the text size the user chose.
    - Never shortening a title in a list: one long name makes its row tower over the list.
  - Becomes true: at any text size every control is on screen, and every name can be read, in its list up to two lines and in full on its own page.
  - Drawings: 7

- PD11. A deck stands on its own, with no subject above it. The library is one list of decks, and "Your library" alone stands above a deck's page. New deck asks for the deck's name and its fields and nothing above them, and the steps to a first practice hold no step for a subject. A deck's name is unique among the user's decks. The Practice page narrows by deck only. Wherever a screen named a subject, it names the deck alone. The subjects the user has made go away and every deck stays. A deck whose name another of the user's decks also has takes its former subject's name in parentheses after its own, as "Verbs (Spanish)". A subject's description and picture go with it.
  - Facts:
    - A subject is a name, a description and a picture, and every deck must sit in exactly one.
    - A subject gives the user a page, a one-tap practice of its decks, a mastery over its cards, and one more kind to tell apart in every address.
    - A practice of several decks is named, kept and re-run in one tap, under PD3 and PD5.
    - Anki has no kind above the deck, and Quizlet's folders are optional.
  - Rejected:
    - Subjects as today: a required kind, a required step and a page, for a topic's mastery alone.
    - Optional labels on a deck: nothing required, but still a kind to tell apart, and no need for grouping shown yet.
    - A subject a deck may or may not have: keeps the kind and adds a "no subject" state to every list.
  - Becomes true: a first practice has no step for a subject, every screen shows one kind fewer, and a topic has no mastery. This supersedes the subject's half of PDR 022, changes PDR 016's steps and PDR 017's row above a deck's page, and empties the vocabulary's Subject area.
  - Drawings: 2, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12

- PD12. A deck's configuration, its prompt and answer sides, is a part of a practice, not a thing of its own. New practice opens on "Choose decks": the user searches the decks by name and picks each from the list, and each picked deck shows below, flat, with a cross at its top right that takes it out. One screen per picked deck follows, in the order picked, headed "Configure:" and the deck's name and holding that deck's board; a field left in Not used is left out of the practice. Every New practice screen holds the practice's Name below its title. The button at the foot reads "Next", or "Create" on the last deck's screen, and is greyed while no deck is picked. Every screen after "Choose decks" has Back. A deck's configuration in a new practice starts as the configuration that deck was last practiced with, or, for a deck never practiced, as its first field on the prompt side and every other field on the answer side. A configuration is made with the practice, kept with it and gone with it, and has no name of its own. A deck's page lists its cards and nothing else. The configurations the user has made as things of their own go away, and every practice keeps the configurations it was made with.
  - Facts:
    - A configuration is a thing of its own with a name, kept on the deck's Configurations tab, made and edited on two forms of its own, picked one per deck on New practice, and copied into the practice at its start.
    - The user meets the configuration in eleven sentences, three of them failures.
    - A practice is named under PD3, re-run in one tap under PD5 and started again from its summary under PDR 026, each time with the configurations it was practiced with.
    - Quizlet keeps how a set is asked as an option of the session, remembered for the set.
    - A deck's board for 12 fields fills more than a phone screen.
  - Rejected:
    - Configurations as today: a kind every user pays for on every screen, for the sake of users who ask one deck several ways.
    - One configuration per deck, kept like its fields: a deck can be asked only one way without a second copy of it and its cards.
    - Configurations trimmed, with no tab and no typed name: still a kind to pick and to tell apart.
    - Each ticked deck's configuration in one line, its board in a sheet: moving a field opens a second sheet over the first.
    - Every ticked deck's board always open: one large deck fills the page.
    - Each ticked deck's board unfolded under it: the decks below are pushed out of view.
    - One screen of decks, fields and sides: choosing fields repeats what Not used already does.
    - Decks ticked by checkboxes under an "All decks" filter: a filter cannot narrow a list that is itself the decks.
    - Name on "Choose decks" alone: the screens differ at their top.
    - "Next (configure Ports)" on the button: too long to read well in the bar.
  - Becomes true: a first practice is three steps, a deck's page has no tabs, every sentence about a configuration leaves the app, and each picked deck gets a screen of its own on New practice, holding its board. This supersedes PDR 014, moves PDR 008's board, and removes the Configurations list PDR 003 gave a button.
  - Drawings: 7, 8, 11

- PD13. Every deck, field and practice carries a mark of its kind wherever its name appears: in a row, a heading, a line, a picker, a form, a chip on the board and a label on a card. A deck's mark is a stack of cards and a practice's a flash of lightning, both drawn by the app as outlines in the color of the words beside them, ink or grey, never a color of their own. A field's mark is its name in square brackets, as "[Command]". A mark never stands beside the word that names its own kind, as in "Add deck", "Add practice", "Add field" or the top bar's New sheet. A card carries no mark. The play button keeps its triangle.
  - Facts:
    - A practice's row names the practice over its decks, New practice names each deck over the fields on its board, and a deck's page names fields on every card; everywhere else a name stands in a list of its own kind.
    - Every icon the app draws is an outline of one stroke weight, and PDR 015 gives ink to what is said and grey to what names or places something.
    - A character the app's font lacks is drawn by the device's fallback font, differently on each device, and "⚡" is a yellow emoji on most phones.
  - Rejected:
    - Marks typed as characters, "#" for a deck, "▶" for a practice: the stack and the flash the user chose have no character in the app's font that looks the same on every device.
    - Both marks filled in ink: a filled stack merges into one shape, and a solid mark draws the eye before the name.
    - Brackets only where a field stands beside names of other kinds: the mark would mean one thing on the board and nothing on a card.
    - Telling the kinds apart by their place in a line: the user must count the names, and with one or two names there is nothing to count.
    - A color per kind: fails anyone who cannot tell the colors apart, and takes PDR 015 from eight meanings to more.
    - A kind's word before every name: three extra words on every line, and the slowest pattern in NN/g's study of list indicators.
    - The mark beside its kind's word as well: two glyphs before one word say the same thing twice, and the count or heading beside every list already names the kind of its rows.
  - Becomes true: the user reads a name's kind off the name itself, on every screen, without counting or color. This extends PDR 015 with no new color and PDR 020 with no new shape, and gives the vocabulary a row for each mark.
  - Drawings: 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 12

- PD14. A practice's configurations are set on New practice and nowhere else. Edit practice holds the practice's name and "Delete practice" and nothing more. A user who wants a deck asked another way makes a new practice. A practice no card of which was rated has not practiced its decks, so a deleted or unstarted practice sets no deck's starting configuration.
  - Facts:
    - PD12 sets a deck's configuration on New practice and starts it from the configuration the deck was last practiced with.
    - PD3 gives Edit practice the practice's name and "Delete practice".
    - A practice exists, with "Start practice" on its page, before any card is seen.
  - Rejected:
    - Configurations changeable on Edit practice until the first card is rated: a second shape of Edit practice for a moment few users meet.
    - Configurations changeable at any time: one practice asks its cards two ways, and its summary speaks of it as one thing.
    - The board shown but fixed once a card is rated: a greyed board, which PD6 and PDR 015 avoid.
  - Becomes true: Edit practice stays as PD3 gives it, and a wrong configuration is put right by deleting the practice and making it again, which starts from the configuration the deck was last practiced with, not from the one just deleted.
  - Drawings: 4
