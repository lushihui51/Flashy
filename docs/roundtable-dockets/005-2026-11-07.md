- LPD1. On Home, a run's row opens with a small outlined block holding the run's mark and its day, as LPD6 writes it; beneath stand the practice's name in bold, then its decks as LPD18 joins them, then an active run's bar. Nothing is indented. The play button is round, a triangle inside a circle. Tapping the row anywhere but its play button opens the run's details beneath it, with "Hide", which closes them. A run's details list each of its decks by deck. An active run's details add how many of its cards are passed, failed, retrying and unseen. A completed run's details add "N of M cards" and the five change counts its summary shows.
  - Facts:
    - The row alone names the run and its practice, not how it went.
    - A flash and a name on a run's row said "practice", while the bar and the details said "run".
    - A date beside a name reads as "made on" as easily as "run on".
  - Rejected:
    - The decks alone in the details: the user cannot see how far an active run has gone, or what a completed one did.
    - Details holding "Open practice" and "Delete practice": the play button already starts it, and deleting from Home puts a lasting act beside a passing glance.
    - The day in grey beneath the practice's name with the flash kept: the row opens like a practice's.
    - The day first and the name beneath, indented or not: Home becomes a column of dates.
    - The mark and day as a grey line with no block: the sign then weighs less than the name it marks.
    - The practice's name and the decks with their fonts swapped: the decks become the row's second-loudest line.
  - Becomes true: the user can see what a run holds and how it went without leaving Home, and a run is the one thing on any screen that wears a dated block.
  - Drawings: 2, 12, 17
